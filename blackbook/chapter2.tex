\chapter{LITERATURE REVIEW}

This chapter reviews sources related to wholesale trading, business record keeping, web-based inventory systems, inventory planning, and domain-specific AI assistance. Each source-focused section explains the source's main contribution and identifies the concept that is relevant to the system requirements. Project-specific development methods and results are discussed later in Chapters 3 and 4.

\section{North American Industry Classification System: Wholesale Trade \cite{naics_2022_wholesale_trade}}
The North American Industry Classification System defines wholesale trade as an intermediate stage in the distribution of merchandise. Wholesalers sell or arrange the purchase and sale of goods for resale, durable non-consumer goods, and raw or intermediate materials. This definition distinguishes a reseller from a manufacturer and shows that wholesale work connects an upstream supplier side with a downstream customer side.

The applicable concept is the two-sided trading flow. A suitable information system must connect products and stock with supplier purchases, customer sales, and the financial records created by those transactions. Treating purchasing and selling as parts of one flow makes it possible to follow both the movement of goods and the related money.

\section{Qtier-Rapor: Managing Spreadsheet Systems and Improving Corporate Performance, Compliance and Governance \cite{bishop2008spreadsheet}}
Bishop discusses how organizations use spreadsheets to fill gaps in their main administrative systems. Spreadsheets are flexible and quick to create, but larger spreadsheet collections can be difficult to control because formulas, versions, ownership, and review procedures may be inconsistent.

The applicable concept is controlled, centralized record keeping. Product, transaction, and finance data are easier to review when they share defined fields, validation rules, access controls, and a common database instead of being divided among unrelated spreadsheets. This concept supports traceability and reduces the risk of conflicting versions of the same business record.

\section{Sole Traders Still Not Prepared for Digital Tax Returns in April \cite{times_sage_sole_traders_2025}}
The article reports Sage research on sole traders who still rely on spreadsheets, bank statements, and paper records for financial administration. It describes the continuing gap between manual record keeping and structured digital processes in small-business environments.

The applicable concept is gradual digital support for routine work. A small-business system should organize records without requiring users to understand complex accounting software. Clear workflows for stock, purchasing, sales, billing, and payment follow-up can make operational information easier to find while reducing repeated manual entry.

\section{A Full-Stack Web Solution for Online FMCG Management System Using MERN: Design, Implementation, and Evaluation \cite{paper_1}}
Mittal and Arora present a full-stack web solution for fast-moving consumer goods management. The described system combines inventory control, order processing, stock updates, billing, and user roles through a browser-based interface.

The applicable concepts are shared web access and integration between operational modules. A browser interface allows different staff roles to use the same current records, while a server-side application can coordinate stock changes, order status, billing, and access control. These concepts are relevant regardless of the specific frontend or backend framework used to implement them.

\section{Essentials of Inventory Management \cite{paper_2}}
Muller explains that inventory management is broader than counting the quantity physically available. It includes demand, replenishment, stock handling, inventory value, and the decisions required to balance availability with cost.

The applicable concepts are stock visibility, demand awareness, replenishment review, and inventory valuation. Useful inventory information should therefore show not only on-hand quantity, but also expected demand, incoming supply, reorder needs, average cost, stock value, and the transaction history behind those values.

\section{Inventory and Production Management in Supply Chains \cite{silver2016inventory}}
Silver, Pyke, and Thomas describe inventory planning as a connected set of decisions involving demand, replenishment, lead time, service level, stockout risk, and cost. The text explains why an inventory position cannot be judged only from the quantity currently on hand.

The applicable concepts are demand during lead time, safety stock, reorder level, and order-up-to planning. Considering these values together provides a traceable basis for deciding when additional stock may be needed and how current demand and confirmed incoming supply affect that decision.

\section{Inventory and Business Calculation Basis}
The system applies the reviewed inventory concepts through a limited set of calculation groups. Equations in this section define quantity normalization, transaction totals, stock availability, replenishment indicators, and finance balances. The reorder calculations are based on demand, lead-time, safety-stock, and inventory-value concepts described by Muller and by Silver, Pyke, and Thomas \cite{paper_2,silver2016inventory}.

\subsection{Unit Conversion and Line Amounts}
Products may be purchased or sold in units that differ from their stock-counting unit. Equation~\ref{eq:base-quantity} converts a transaction quantity into the product's base unit so that all stock movements use the same measure.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:base-quantity}
\text{Base Quantity} = \text{Quantity} \times \text{Conversion Factor}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:discounted-amount} calculates a line amount after one or more percentage discounts. Each discount percentage is limited to the range from 0\% to 100\%. For purchases, unit price represents unit cost; for sales and quotations, it represents selling price.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discounted-amount}
\text{Discounted Amount} =
\text{Quantity} \times \text{Unit Price} \times
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1 - \frac{\text{Discount Percent}_i}{100}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{VAT Calculation}
The general value-added tax rate used for taxable goods and services in Thailand is 7\% \cite{thailand_revenue_vat}. Equation~\ref{eq:vat-calculation} shows how the same rate is applied when VAT is already included in an item total and when VAT must be added to an item total.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vat-calculation}
\begin{aligned}
r &= 0.07,\\
\text{Included Before VAT} &= \frac{\text{Item Total}}{1+r},\\
\text{Included VAT} &= \text{Item Total}-\text{Included Before VAT},\\
\text{Excluded VAT} &= \text{Item Total}\times r,\\
\text{Excluded Grand Total} &= \text{Item Total}+\text{Excluded VAT}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Current Stock}
Stock is derived from completed stock movements rather than entered as an independent manual number. As shown in Equation~\ref{eq:current-stock}, received purchase quantities add stock, while sale quantities in stock-committing statuses reduce it.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:current-stock}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Current Stock} = \max(0,\;&\text{Received Purchase Base Quantity}\\
&-\text{Committed Sale Base Quantity}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Only purchase items marked as received contribute to available stock. Sale items reduce stock when they are packed, shipped, or delivered. Pending sales remain visible as demand but do not reduce current stock until they reach a stock-committing status.

\subsection{Stock Validation and Allocation}
Before inventory is issued for a sale, the requested quantity is compared with the quantity available. A request that exceeds available stock is rejected before the transaction can commit the stock movement.

When stock is available, the quantity is assigned to received purchase batches. The default method uses first-in, first-out allocation, so older available stock is used before newer stock. Equation~\ref{eq:allocation-quantity} limits the quantity taken from a batch to the smaller of the remaining sale requirement and the quantity still available in that batch.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation-quantity}
\text{Allocation Quantity} = \min(\text{Remaining Sale Quantity},\text{ Batch Available Quantity})
\end{equation}

Manual allocation may be used when a specific physical batch is selected. The selected batch must contain the same product, must already be received, and must have enough unallocated quantity.

\subsection{Inventory Dashboard Calculations}
The first calculation group prepares the cost and demand values used for reorder review. Equation~\ref{eq:reorder-components} defines average unit cost, average daily demand, demand during lead time, safety stock, and the calculated reorder level.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reorder-components}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Average Unit Cost} &= \frac{\text{Received Purchase Value}}{\text{Received Purchase Units}},\\
\text{Average Daily Demand} &= \frac{\text{Sales History Units}}{\text{Sales History Days}},\\
\text{Lead-Time Demand} &= \text{Average Daily Demand}\times\text{Average Lead Time},\\
\text{Safety Stock} &= \text{Average Daily Demand}\times\text{Safety Stock Days},\\
\text{Reorder Level} &= \left\lceil\text{Lead-Time Demand}+\text{Safety Stock}\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:restock-and-value} then uses the reorder values with pending demand, oversold quantity, available stock, and pending purchases. It produces a recommended restock quantity and an estimated stock value for review.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restock-and-value}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Order-up-to Level} &= \text{Reorder Level}+\left\lceil\text{Lead-Time Demand}\right\rceil,\\
\text{Recommended Restock} &= \max(0,\;\text{Order-up-to Level}+\text{Pending Sales}\\
&\quad+\text{Oversold Units}-\text{Available Stock}-\text{Pending Purchases}),\\
\text{Stock Value} &= \text{Available Stock}\times\text{Average Unit Cost}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

These indicators support reorder review rather than automatically placing an order. Supplier cost history can then be used to compare possible replenishment sources.

\subsection{Purchase Payable and Payment Batch Calculations}
Cancelled purchase lines should not remain payable, while the original purchase document should remain readable. Equation~\ref{eq:purchase-payment-totals} adjusts the payable amount according to the value of non-cancelled lines and calculates a payment batch from the lines included in that batch.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:purchase-payment-totals}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Payable Total} &= \text{Grand Total}\times
\frac{\text{Non-cancelled Line Amounts}}{\text{All Line Amounts}},\\
\text{Payment Batch Total} &= \sum \text{Payment Batch Line Amount}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Unpaid payment lines can be updated when the related payable amount changes. Paid lines remain unchanged because they represent a completed payment record.

\subsection{Billing Note and Credit Note Calculations}
Credit notes reduce the amount still expected from a customer. Equation~\ref{eq:billing-net-amount} calculates the net billing amount after active credit adjustments are deducted.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:billing-net-amount}
\text{Billing Net Amount} = \text{Billing Note Total}-\text{Active Credit Note Total}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cashflow and Net Position}
Open receivables and payables are grouped by due date for cashflow review. Equation~\ref{eq:open-net-position} shows whether open customer receivables are greater or lower than open supplier payables.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:open-net-position}
\text{Open Net Position} = \text{Open Accounts Receivable}-\text{Open Accounts Payable}
\end{equation}

An open item is overdue when its expected or planned payment date is earlier than the current date and it has not been completed.

\subsection{Quotation Validity}
Quotation validity is determined from the quotation date and the selected validity period. Calendar-day validity adds every day, while business-day validity skips weekends. A quotation may also be stored without a validity end date. This rule is described in text because it is a date-processing rule rather than an inventory calculation.

Together, Equations~\ref{eq:base-quantity} to~\ref{eq:open-net-position} provide the calculation basis for four purposes: consistent transaction quantities, correct document totals, controlled stock and replenishment review, and clear receivable or payable balances. Their implementation method is described in Chapter 3, and their validation results are reported in Chapter 4.

\section{Aisha: A Custom AI Library Chatbot Using the ChatGPT API \cite{paper_3}}
Lappalainen and Narayanan describe a custom chatbot for a university library. The chatbot uses prepared institutional information and a controlled application context so that answers remain focused on the library domain instead of becoming unrestricted general conversation.

The applicable concept is domain-limited question answering. An assistant connected to operational data should define the topics it supports, keep record-changing actions outside the chat interface, and clearly reject or redirect unsupported requests. This boundary is especially important when the available information includes stock or financial records.

OpenAI's structured-output guidance describes the use of a JSON Schema to constrain response fields \cite{openai_structured_outputs}. The applicable concept is a predictable response contract: a system can require separate fields for an answer, a conclusion, and highlights while keeping factual values in application-prepared data.

\section{Summary}
The reviewed sources establish the main concepts used by the Inventory Management System. Wholesale-trade classification explains the connected supplier-to-customer flow. The spreadsheet and small-business sources support centralized records and straightforward digital workflows. The web-based FMCG study supports integrated browser-based modules and role-aware access.

The inventory-management books provide the basis for stock availability, lead-time demand, safety stock, reorder review, and inventory value. The chatbot and structured-output references support a limited read-only assistant with predictable response sections. These concepts are applied through the architecture and methods described in Chapter 3 and evaluated through the results in Chapter 4.
